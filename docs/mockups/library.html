Library (#/library) — single column, drill-down, breadcrumb spine

Phase 2 of the Tyndale cultural layer. No sidebar: one surface at a time — start → a route's index → an article — under a centred, linkable breadcrumb that is the navigation stack. The Start crumb is the way home, so there is no separate return button.

Try it: pick Dictionary → B · open Beast · follow two or three Where this leads doors · then hit ⁂ View path map. It draws your route as a spine with every branch you didn’t take hanging off it — click one and the trail rewinds to that step and continues from there. Also try ✦ Wander in, search revelation (one query, three datasets), and Books → Revelation for the hub. Toggle light/dark top-right.

Every title, body, cross-reference, theme, profile, count and citation figure is real, queried from data/bible.db. Nothing is invented — including the warts listed at the bottom.

DeepVerse

What this asserts

  • The breadcrumb is the stack. There is no separate navigation state — each surface pushes a crumb, and clicking a crumb truncates back to it. That is also what makes browser back/forward work for free, and why Start needs no companion “return” button.
  • The path map is the breadcrumb with its branches shown. Same stack, drawn as a spine: each step is a crumb, and each article step fans out the cross-references you passed over. Clicking one rewinds the trail to that step and continues from there — so the breadcrumb stays a truthful account of the route, never a log of every click. A solid spine link means the step followed a cross-reference; a dashed one means you arrived by search, a route, or ✦ Wander in.
    This replaced a per-article “neighbourhood” graph, which looked good and did nothing the doors row didn't already do better.
  • Search is a surface, not a mode. Typing pushes a search crumb; clearing the field pops it. One query reaches all four routes, because making you first guess which route holds your answer would tax the primary objective.
  • “Where this leads” is the rabbit hole. The prose links stay, but every article also ends with its outbound cross-references as explicit doors — and when there are none, it says so and offers a way onward rather than leaving you at a silent dead end. Try Shepherd via ✦ Wander in.
  • Each route's index is shaped to its data — a letter grid for 6,010, book-grouped for 298, A–Z for 125, a 66-cell grid opening onto hubs. Four datasets, four shapes, not four copies of one list.
  • Scripture books read as names, except in the A–Z. Tyndale files them inverted (Revelation, Book of), which is correct for alphabetical browse and wrong everywhere else, so the index keeps the source form and every other surface shows Book of Revelation. The de-inversion runs off an exact allowlist of ten phrases covering 62 articles — never a general rule, because the corpus also holds unmarked inversions (Paulus, Sergius, Calf, Golden) that are indistinguishable from alternate spellings (Elect, Election, Zidon*, Zidonian*).
  • Cross-references are linkified only inside a See … clause. Calf, Clay, Hour, Evening and Command are all real article titles; linkifying titles wherever they occur in prose would make every paragraph a minefield.

Warts on display, all real

  • 5% of cross-reference targets don't exist. Open Antichrist: False Christs, False Messiahs and Prophets, False are named by Tyndale and absent from the corpus. They stay plain text in the prose and are listed honestly under the doors instead of 404-ing.
  • Mark of the Beast is not a title. It is the second headword of Mark of God*, Mark of the Beast, and resolves only via the unique-title-segment tier.
  • sort_title is not unique — 131 colliding groups. The Dictionary index shows Baal (Idol) / Baal (Person) / Baal* (Place) in full, because sorting by sort_title while displaying it would print “Baal” three identical times.
  • 13 charts and textboxes have no host article and are unreachable anywhere in the app today. The Dictionary index lists them.
